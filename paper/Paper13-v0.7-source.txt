A Lattice Quantum Field Theory Test of the Static Escrow Postulate:
1+1D and 3+1D Falsification with Modular-Hamiltonian Partial Survival
Grant L. Whitmer III
Windstorm Institute, Fort Ann, New York 12828, USA
(Dated: May 2026; Draft v0.7)

ABSTRACT
We test the static gravitational entropy escrow postulate Sₙₘ = |U_grav|/T_Unruh of
Whitmer [1] using lattice quantum field theory. In 1+1 dimensions, a 295-point parameter
scan of a free massless scalar at lattice sizes up to N = 3000 finds that the dimensionless
ratio R = S_ent/Sₙₘ spans 10.56 orders of magnitude across the (L,m) grid; the massinduced entanglement ΔS = S_ent − S_vac is uniformly negative; and the result is Nconverged to four decimal places. In 3+1 dimensions, an N³-site scan over N ∈ {10, 12,
14, 16, 18, 20} confirms the Bombelli–Srednicki area law (S_vac/N² → 0.0228) and
shows that R has no finite continuum limit because the bipartition entropy is dominated
by the area-law vacuum term, while the mass-induced ratio R_Δ = ΔS/Sₙₘ is bounded by
10⁻³ across the grid. Two independent code paths agree to five decimals, ruling out
implementation artifacts. Mutual information I(M₁:M₂) between regions surrounding the
masses decays as L⁻⁴, opposite to the linear growth required by the postulate. The
modular Hamiltonian content ΔK, evaluated under the Bisognano–Wichmann conjecture,
exhibits the right sign and approximately recovers the BW linear asymptote ΔK ∝ d1 in
1+1D within a small-d1 window (d1 ∈ [2, 6] at m=1), with prefactor approximately 1/30
of the literal BW value. At larger d1 the local exponent decreases smoothly into a
sublinear decay tail; the previously-published v0.4/v0.5 figure of "ΔK ∝ L^{0.7}" is here
corrected to a regime-dependent characterization, with the single-power-law exponent
identified as a fitting artifact across a smooth crossover. The literal bipartition-entropy
reading of the postulate is ruled out in both 1+1D and 3+1D; the modular-content
interpretation is approximately correct in 1+1D within the small-d1 BW regime, with a
calculable suppression factor as the remaining open question. A companion paper [11]
reports that the 3+1D modular content does not recover the BW asymptote within the d1
range the lattice can resolve, with ΔK peaked at d1 ≈ 2 and decaying as d1^{−2 to −3}
for larger d1, indicating that BW recovery is dimension-dependent or, equivalently, that
the rate of approach to the continuum BW asymptote is substantially slower in 3+1D than
in 1+1D. The framework's horizon-limit recoveries (Bekenstein–Hawking entropy via
surface gravity) are independent of these flat-space tests.

I. INTRODUCTION
The Gravitational Entropy Escrow framework [1] proposes that gravitational binding energy is
identified with entropy held in escrow against the local Unruh temperature,
S_esc = |U_grav| / T_Unruh ,

(1)

where U_grav is the Newtonian binding energy of a configuration and T_Unruh = ħa/(2πck_B) is the
Unruh temperature corresponding to the local acceleration a. For two equal point masses m at separation
L, the prediction simplifies in lattice natural units (c = ħ = k_B = 1, lattice spacing = 1) to
S_esc / k_B = 2π·m·L ,

(2)

with G_eff and the source mass cancelling identically. The framework recovers Newton's law via the
Verlinde construction [2], Bekenstein–Hawking entropy at horizons via direct integration of Eq. (1) with
surface gravity, and the deep-MOND acceleration scale a₀ = c²√(Λ/3)/(2π) from the de Sitter horizon
temperature floor.
The central claim of [1] is interpretive: that |U_grav|/T_Unruh has the dimensions of and represents
an entropy held in escrow against the binding. Section 7.6 of [1] explicitly identifies a derivation of Eq.
(1) from quantum field theory first principles as the natural next step. A companion note [3] ruled out the
natural-looking covariant local-current extension of Eq. (1) as a notational reformulation of the saturated
Bekenstein bound [4]. With the dynamical extension closed off, the static identification (1) becomes the
load-bearing claim that requires independent testing.
This paper reports such a test. We compute several QFT entropy quantities of a free massless scalar in
the presence of two localized mass distributions and compare to the prediction (2). The tests are
performed in 1+1D using Peschel's exact formula for Gaussian states [5] and extended to 3+1D using the
same machinery on cubic lattices. Three independent entropy measures are tested: bipartition
entanglement entropy, mutual information between regions, and modular Hamiltonian content evaluated
under the Bisognano–Wichmann conjecture [6].
The headline result is that the literal identification of S_esc with bipartition entanglement entropy
fails in both 1+1D and 3+1D. The mutual information measure confirms this independently. The modular
Hamiltonian content gives a partial-survival result: right structural form, wrong magnitude. Section II
describes the methodology. Section III describes the validations. Section IV reports the 1+1D result,
Section V the independent confirmations from mutual information and the 3+1D continuum-limit study,
Section VI the modular Hamiltonian partial-survival result. Section VII discusses the falsifiability check.
Sections VIII–X interpret what the test settles, methodological notes, and conclusion.

II. METHODOLOGY
A. Lattice setup (1+1D)
We discretize a free massless scalar field on a 1+1D lattice with N sites, with Dirichlet boundary
conditions at both walls (avoiding the periodic-BC zero mode). The Hamiltonian is
H = (1/2) Σᵢ [πᵢ² + (φᵢ₊₁ − φᵢ)² + mᵢ² φᵢ²] ,

(3)

which writes as H = (1/2)[πᵀπ + φᵀKφ] with
K = 2I − S − Sᵀ + diag(mᵢ²) ,

(4)

where S is the lattice shift operator. Two equal localized masses are placed at sites (N−L)/2 and
(N+L)/2, on opposite sides of the bipartition cut at site N/2 and at distance L/2 each from the cut. Singlesite delta masses are used; Section IV.D verifies that the conclusions are invariant under Gaussian
smearing of the mass profile.
The parameter scan covers N ∈ {100, 300, 1000, 3000}, L ∈ {2, 4, 6, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320, 640}
lattice spacings, and m ∈ [10⁻³, 10²] sampled across orders of magnitude, for 295 distinct (N, L, m)
points.

B. Correlators and Peschel formula
The ground state of (3) is Gaussian, with closed-form correlators
⟨φᵢ φⱼ⟩ = ½ (K⁻¹ᐟ²)ᵢⱼ ,

⟨πᵢ πⱼ⟩ = ½ (K¹ᐟ²)ᵢⱼ .

(5)

Restricting the correlators to subsystem A (sites [0, N/2)) gives the reduced covariances M_φ = ⟨φφᵀ ⟩|
_A and M_π = ⟨ππᵀ⟩|_A. The symmetric product
M_sym = M_φ¹ᐟ² M_π M_φ¹ᐟ²

(6)

has eigenvalues νₖ² ≥ ¼, and the bipartition entanglement entropy follows from Peschel's formula [5]:
S_ent = Σₖ [(νₖ + ½) ln(νₖ + ½) − (νₖ − ½) ln(νₖ − ½)] .

(7)

The numerical procedure is: build K from (4), diagonalize to obtain Eq. (5), restrict to subsystem A,
form M_sym from (6), diagonalize, and apply (7). All operations use double-precision dense linear
algebra.
C. Comparison metrics
Three diagnostic ratios are computed from the lattice measurements and compared to the postulate's
prediction (2):
R_total = S_ent / S_esc ,

(8)

R_Δ = ΔS / S_esc ,

(9)

ΔS ≡ S_ent − S_vac ,

where S_vac is the entanglement entropy at the same N with no localized masses (a function of N and
the boundary condition only). In addition, mutual information I(M₁:M₂) between regions surrounding
each mass and modular Hamiltonian content ΔK are introduced in Sections V and VI respectively. If the
postulate (2) holds with an order-unity prefactor, the diagnostic ratios should be approximately constant
of order unity across the parameter scan.
D. Lattice setup (3+1D)
The 3+1D setup uses a cubic lattice of N³ sites with Dirichlet boundary conditions on all six faces.
Two point masses are placed on the central x-axis at coordinates (i, N/2, N/2) with i = (N±L)/2, with the
bipartition cutting the cube along the (j,k) plane at i = N/2. The K-matrix has shape N³ × N³ with
Laplacian structure plus diagonal mass terms; for N = 20 this is 8000 × 8000.
The 3+1D scan covers N ∈ {10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20}, L ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8}, and m ∈ {0.1, 1.0, 10.0}. Two
independent computational paths were used: a CPU implementation using NumPy with OpenBLAS
multi-threading across 24 cores, and a GPU implementation using torch.linalg.eigh on an NVIDIA RTX
5090. The two paths agree on ΔS at the N = 14 anchor to better than 2×10⁻⁵, providing independent
cross-validation; see Section IX.

B. Heavy-mass Dirichlet limit (V2)
In the limit m → ∞, the localized-mass sites become perfect Dirichlet walls, decoupling the lattice
into independent regions. The bipartition cuts the inner interval [i₁, i₂] of length L at its midpoint.

The escrow prediction (2) was derived symbolically using sympy and verified to be the unique
dimensionally-consistent form after the cancellation of G_eff and the source mass. Per the methodological
lessons of [3], dimensional consistency is necessary but not sufficient — the substantive checks are V1
and V2.

IV. RESULT: BIPARTITION ENTANGLEMENT ENTROPY IN 1+1D
A. Quantitative summary
Across the 295-point (N, L, m) parameter scan in 1+1D:
(i) The diagnostic ratio R_total defined in Eq. (8) ranges from 2.3×10⁻⁹ to 83.3 at N = 1000 — a span
of 10.56 orders of magnitude across the (L, m) grid. The geometric mean |R_total| is approximately
8×10⁻³.
(ii) The mass-induced contribution ΔS = S_ent − S_vac is uniformly negative across all scanned
points. Localized masses reduce entanglement rather than increasing it.
(iii) Results are converged with respect to lattice size to four decimal places for m ≥ 1 across N ∈
{100, 300, 1000, 3000}; the scatter in R is not a finite-size artifact.

establishes that the failure is not a lattice artifact.

B. Why the literal postulate fails: the structural mismatch
The escrow prediction Eq. (2) grows linearly with both m and L without bound, with G_eff and the
source mass exactly cancelling. The free-scalar entanglement entropy across a bipartition behaves very
differently. The vacuum part S_vac scales as (1/6) ln(2N/π) — bounded for fixed N, growing only
logarithmically with system size. The mass-induced part ΔS is bounded above by O(1) nats in absolute
value: localized masses entangle or disentangle modes only within a correlation length ξ ∼ 1/m of
themselves, giving an O(1) entropy contribution that saturates as L → ∞. Heavy localized masses act as
effective Dirichlet walls in the chain, decreasing the bipartition entanglement and giving ΔS < 0.
The mismatch is structural, not a question of a missing prefactor: S_esc is an extensive quantity
proportional to mL; ΔS is intensive and bounded by topology. No constant rescaling, dimensional
adjustment, or modified temperature in (1) can reconcile a bounded quantity with an unbounded one.
C. Robustness against UV artifacts
The result is invariant under the choice of how the localized mass is implemented. Single-site delta
masses and Gaussian-smeared profiles with widths σ ∈ {1, 2, 5} lattice spacings all produce the same
qualitative behavior: ΔS uniformly negative, R_total varying across many orders of magnitude. For m ≤
0.1, ΔS is invariant to smearing to better than 1%. For m ∼ 1 and larger, smearing makes ΔS more
negative — a broader profile is a broader "wall," removing more entanglement. Single-site UV artifacts
are not driving the result.

V. INDEPENDENT CONFIRMATIONS
Two additional tests confirm the negative result of Section IV from independent angles. The first uses
an entirely different entropy measure (mutual information instead of bipartition entanglement); the second
probes whether the failure persists in higher spatial dimensions.
A. Mutual information I(M₁:M₂)
If the prediction (2) were correctly identified with QFT entropy, a natural alternative measure is the
mutual information between regions M₁ and M₂ surrounding the two localized masses,
I(M₁:M₂) = S(M₁) + S(M₂) − S(M₁ ∪ M₂) .

(13)

For free massless scalars in 1+1D, the leading contribution at large separation scales as [10]
I(M₁:M₂) ∼ (w/L)⁴ ,

(14)

with w the width of each region. We measured I(M₁:M₂) for w = 4 lattice sites surrounding each mass
and L ∈ {10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320}. The mutual information was found to decay as L⁻⁴ across this range,
while the postulate's prediction grows as 2πmL. The diagnostic ratio R_MI = I(M₁:M₂)/S_esc thus spans
more than 12 orders of magnitude across the parameter scan, in the opposite direction to what the
postulate requires. This is an independent confirmation that the literal postulate's linear growth is
incompatible with QFT entropy measures in flat space.
B. 3+1D continuum-limit study
The 3+1D bipartition entanglement entropy was computed across N ∈ {10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20} for the
(L, m) grid described in Section II.D. The vacuum area-law validation (Table II) confirmed the lattice
implementation. The mass-induced results yield a structurally sharper failure than 1+1D.
Table V shows R_total at three representative (L, m) pairs across N. The diagnostic ratio R_total has
no finite continuum limit: it grows as N² across the entire (L, m) grid, with
R_total · S_esc = S_vac

(15)

VI. MODULAR HAMILTONIAN CONTENT: A PARTIAL-SURVIVAL RESULT
A. Bisognano–Wichmann motivation
The Bisognano–Wichmann theorem [6] states that the modular Hamiltonian K_A of a Lorentzinvariant quantum field theory restricted to a half-space is, in vacuum,
K_BW = 2π ∫ x_⊥ T₀₀(x) dᵈx ,

(16)

where x_⊥ is the perpendicular distance from the entangling surface and T₀₀ is the energy density.
For a localized energy m at perpendicular distance L from the cut, the linear-response prediction is
ΔK_BW ≃ 2π·m·L ,

(17)

which is exactly the form of the escrow prediction (2). The structural match is striking: both are linear
in m and L with the same numerical coefficient, in the same regime (linear response to a localized energy
at perpendicular distance L from a half-space cut). This raises the possibility that the postulate's correct
identification is with modular Hamiltonian content rather than entanglement entropy.
B. Lattice implementation
We computed
ΔK = ⟨K_A^vac⟩_perturbed − ⟨K_A^vac⟩_vacuum

(18)

across the same 1+1D lattice grid as Section IV, where K_A^vac is the modular Hamiltonian of the
vacuum reduced to subsystem A and the perturbed state is the ground state with localized masses on. The
implementation uses the Williamson normal form of the vacuum covariance matrix; details follow.

Let M_φ and M_π be the reduced φ-φ and π-π covariances on A. Diagonalize the symmetric product
M_φ¹ᐟ² M_π M_φ¹ᐟ² = V diag(νₖ²) Vᵀ, and define modular eigenvalues
εₖ = ln[(νₖ + ½)/(νₖ − ½)] .

(19)

The modular Hamiltonian quadratic kernels in the (φ, π) basis are then
Q = M_φ⁻¹ᐟ² V diag(εₖνₖ) Vᵀ M_φ⁻¹ᐟ² ,

(20)

P = M_φ¹ᐟ² V diag(εₖ/νₖ) Vᵀ M_φ¹ᐟ² ,

(21)

such that K_A_quad = (1/2)φᵀQφ + (1/2)πᵀPπ plus a constant offset fixed by ⟨K_A⟩_vac = S_vac.
The expectation value of K_A_quad in any other Gaussian state with covariances M'_φ, M'_π is then
⟨K_A_quad⟩ = ½ tr(Q M'_φ) + ½ tr(P M'_π) .

(22)

The implementation was internally validated by three checks: (i) relative-entropy positivity, S_rel =
ΔK + S_vac − S_pert ≥ 0 verified for all scanned states (this is the strict consequence of relative-entropy
non-negativity and is a sanity check on the modular kernel construction independent of whether the BW
conjecture holds); (ii) modal-vs-original-basis cross-check, with ⟨K_quad⟩_vac computed two
independent ways agreeing to six decimal places; (iii) constant-offset consistency between the modular
Hamiltonian's quadratic part and the vacuum entanglement entropy.
An initial implementation produced relative-entropy violations of order 10⁴, immediately flagging an
error in the Williamson decomposition; correction yielded order-unity values with positivity verified
throughout. See Section IX.
C. Result: right form, wrong magnitude
The lattice measurement of ΔK confirms the BW structural form: ΔK is positive, grows with L, and is
approximately linear in m for moderate values. This is the correct sign and the correct scaling direction
predicted by the escrow postulate when reinterpreted as modular content. Two quantitative discrepancies
emerge.
First, the L-scaling is not a single power law. The published v0.4 of this paper reported a singlepower-law fit ΔK ∝ L^{0.7±0.05} at (m=1, N=1000); a re-examination using sliding-window fits across
the same data shows that this single exponent was an artifact of fitting across a smooth crossover. The
local exponent, computed in 5-point sliding windows over d1 (the perpendicular distance of the mass in
subsystem A to the cut, which equals L/2 in the symmetric two-mass placement), evolves continuously
from
ΔK ∝ d1^{1.02±0.05}

[d1 ∈ [2, 6]]

(23)

at small d1 — indistinguishable from the BW linear asymptote — down through intermediate values
to
ΔK ∝ d1^{0.5±0.04}

[d1 ∈ [16, 64]]

(23a)

in the decay tail. The published "0.7" was an effective exponent obtained by fitting a single power
law across both regimes; it is not a universal scaling. The robust statements supported by the data are: (i)
the BW linear asymptote ΔK ∝ d1 is approximately recovered at small d1 in 1+1D, with a suppressed
prefactor; (ii) the crossover into a sublinear decay tail is smooth, not abrupt; (iii) the local exponent at any
given window depends on which window is fit. We retract the v0.4 framing of "sublinear scaling" as a
universal description and replace it with the regime-dependent characterization above.

Second, the magnitude is too small. In the small-d1 BW regime [d1 ∈ [2, 6], m=1, N=1000], the
prefactor ΔK / (2π·m·d1) is approximately 0.030–0.037, corresponding to a suppression factor of roughly
1/30 relative to the literal BW prediction. The closely-related v0.4 figure of "approximately 55× smaller
at L=4, m=1" came from the two-mass protocol where the relevant variable is d1 = L/2 = 2; under either
convention the magnitude suppression is on the order of 30× to 55× depending on which point in the scan
is chosen. For most of the (L, m) grid at heavier mass the apparent ratio drops further (down to 10⁻⁶ in
the heavy-mass / large-L regime), but this is largely because heavy mass takes the system outside the
linear-response regime where the BW formula was derived; ΔK saturates rather than continuing to grow
as 2π·m·d1. The clean BW comparison is at moderate mass (m ≲ 1) and moderate d1 (d1 ∈ [2, 8]), where
the linear asymptote is recovered with a suppressed prefactor.
D. Interpretation
The modular Hamiltonian result in 1+1D is consistent with the BW asymptote at small d1, with a
suppressed prefactor. Specifically, in the regime d1 ∈ [2, 6] at m = 1, the local exponent is +1.02±0.05
(indistinguishable from the BW linear scaling ΔK ∝ d1) and the prefactor is approximately 0.030 of the
literal BW value 2π·m·d1. The smooth crossover into a sublinear decay tail at d1 ≳ 8 reflects geometric
corrections to the BW half-space assumption: the lattice is not a true Rindler wedge, the boundary
conditions are Dirichlet rather than free, and at d1 comparable to subsystem-A depth the half-space
approximation breaks down. The decay tail is therefore consistent with a finite-size deformation of BW
rather than a fundamental failure. Determining whether the prefactor 1/30 has a calculable origin (e.g.,
from boundary-condition corrections, from the lattice regularization, or from a missing dimensiondependent geometric factor) and whether the BW asymptote can be cleanly extracted in the limit of a true
half-chain bipartition is left for future work. The result we report here is that the BW modular
Hamiltonian asymptote is qualitatively correct in 1+1D within a finite small-d1 window; this is closer to a
partial confirmation than v0.4 / v0.5 framings of the same data suggested.
The dimensional dependence of this result is investigated in a companion paper [11], which extends
the same Williamson-decomposition methodology to a 3+1D cubic lattice. The 3+1D result is
qualitatively different from the 1+1D result reported here: the BW linear asymptote is not recovered
within the d1 range the lattice can resolve, and ΔK is peaked at d1 ≈ 2 with a power-law decay ΔK ∝
d1^{−2 to −3} for d1 ≥ 3. At matched mass m = 1 (within the linear-response regime where the BW
comparison is strictly meaningful), the 3+1D peak ratio ΔK/(2π·m·d1) ≈ 0.0026 is approximately 14×
smaller than the 1+1D peak ratio ≈ 0.037 reported in this paper, so 1+1D is closer to BW than 3+1D in
both qualitative scaling and absolute prefactor. The companion paper notes that the rate of approach to the
continuum BW asymptote may genuinely be slower in 3+1D (where the conformal-symmetry group of
the massless free scalar is finite-dimensional) than in 1+1D (where it is the infinite-dimensional Virasoro
algebra), so 3+1D BW recovery at much larger d1 inaccessible to the present lattice cannot be strictly
ruled out. We refer the reader to [11] for the full 3+1D analysis and the comparative discussion.
On the calculable origin of the 1/30 prefactor in 1+1D, one candidate explanation is worth flagging as
a target for follow-up theoretical work. The Bisognano–Wichmann formula K_A^vac = 2π ∫ x_ ⊥ T_00
dV is an infrared / long-wavelength prediction valid in the continuum where Lorentz invariance is exact.
A localized lattice mass perturbation with m ≈ 1 in lattice units corresponds to a correlation length ξ ∼
1/m ∼ 1 lattice spacing, which is a UV-scale perturbation by the lattice's own measure. In this regime the
BW formula's continuum derivation no longer strictly applies; the effective lattice modular Hamiltonian is
expected to differ from the BW formula by a regulator-dependent prefactor that depends on the ratio of
correlation length to lattice spacing. The 1/30 suppression observed at small d1 may reflect this UV/IR
mismatch rather than a fundamental modification of the postulate, and could in principle be calculated
from first principles by computing the lattice modular Hamiltonian directly in the continuum scaling
limit. We do not pursue this calculation here but identify it as a sharp theoretical question that would
either remove or reify the apparent suppression. A second candidate is that the Dirichlet boundary

conditions used for the lattice differ from the free boundary conditions assumed in the standard BW
derivation; the difference may contribute a calculable boundary-correction factor.
We do not adjudicate among these readings. The result is reported as the most promising direction for
future modification of the postulate, and as a sharp constraint on what such modifications must satisfy.

VII. FALSIFIABILITY CHECK
A central concern when comparing computed quantities to dimensional-analysis predictions is the
possibility that any apparent agreement (or disagreement) is a tautology fixed by the choice of
dimensional combinations rather than reflecting physical content. We check this directly.
The Unruh temperature T_Unruh varies across the (L, m) grid as T_Unruh ∝ m/L² in lattice units,
spanning approximately five orders of magnitude across the scanned range. If the test were dimensionally
trivial — if any choice of T would produce R ≃ 1 — then the construction would be uninformative
regardless of the result. We verified that no choice of T produces a uniform R ≃ 1 across the parameter
scan; T_Unruh is not privileged in this sense. The 10.56 orders of magnitude variation in R is therefore a
real measurement of the disagreement between the postulate's prediction and the actual QFT
entanglement structure, not an artifact of dimensional rescaling.
This is the analog of the lesson from [3]: a construction that achieves agreement only by choosing the
dimensional combination that produces the desired answer is not an empirical confirmation. The lattice
test passes this falsifiability check; the Section IV result is a genuine quantitative measurement of the
postulate's failure as bipartition entanglement entropy.

VIII. WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
A. What this settles
The literal static escrow postulate (1), taken as a quantitative identification of S_esc with QFT
bipartition entanglement entropy in flat space with point-mass binding, does not survive in either 1+1D or
3+1D. In 1+1D, the qualitative behavior is wrong (sign, scaling, boundedness in N), the magnitude is
wrong (10.56 orders-of-magnitude scatter), and the lattice-size convergence rules out finite-size
explanations. In 3+1D, the bipartition entropy is dominated by the area-law vacuum term and has no
finite continuum limit when divided by the postulate's prediction; the dimensionless mass-induced ratio
R_Δ is bounded by 10⁻³, three orders of magnitude below the postulate's prediction. Two independent
code paths agree to five decimals on the 3+1D calculation, ruling out implementation artifacts. The
mutual information measure confirms the failure from a third independent direction. No constant
rescaling of S_esc by a dimensionless factor or by a modified temperature can repair the disagreement.
B. What this does not settle
The horizon limit is unaffected. The framework's recovery of Bekenstein–Hawking entropy at black
hole horizons [1, §5] goes through surface gravity and the Unruh temperature at the horizon directly, not
through volume-bipartition entanglement. The lattice test does not address the horizon recovery. The
horizon claims of [1] are independent of the present result.
Curved-spacetime regimes are also outside scope. The 3+1D test reported here uses a flat-space
lattice with imported Newtonian binding-energy form. The framework's predictions for genuine curvedspacetime configurations (non-perturbative gravity, full general-relativistic treatment) are not addressed
by this test and require lattice quantum gravity techniques beyond the scope of the present calculation.
The framework's empirical content is unaffected. The empirical predictions of [1] — the constant-a₀
SPARC reanalysis, the deep-MOND asymptote a₀ = c²√(Λ/3)/(2π), and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation
— do not depend on the literal bipartition-entanglement reading of the static postulate. They depend on

the deep-MOND asymptote and the framework's interpretive identification at horizons, neither of which
is refuted by the present result.
Modular-content variants remain partially open. The modular Hamiltonian result of Section VI shows
the BW linear asymptote approximately recovered at small d1 in 1+1D, with prefactor approximately
1/30. The companion paper [11] extends the same methodology to 3+1D and finds that the BW asymptote
is not recovered within the d1 range the lattice can resolve, with ΔK peaked at d1 ≈ 2 and decaying as
d1^{−2 to −3} for d1 ≥ 3. At matched mass (m = 1) the 1+1D peak ratio is also approximately 14× larger
than the 3+1D peak ratio, so 1+1D is closer to BW in both qualitative scaling and absolute prefactor.
Taken together, the two papers show that the modular-content interpretation of the static escrow postulate
has dimension-dependent validity within the lattice sizes presently accessible: approximately correct in
1+1D within a finite small-d1 window; not recovered in 3+1D at the d1 range probed, with the open
question of whether 3+1D BW recovery can be achieved at much larger d1 not currently accessible.
Whether the 1+1D 1/30 prefactor has a calculable origin (boundary-condition corrections, lattice
regularization with UV/IR mismatch, or a missing geometric factor in the postulate) and whether the
3+1D structure can be reconciled with a modified BW form remain open theoretical questions for future
work.
C. Implication for the framework
The framework's interpretive content survives the test. Its quantitative literal interpretation —
specifically, the reading in which |U_grav|/T_Unruh equals the bipartition entanglement entropy of a
quantum field across a surface separating two masses — does not. What survives is dimension-dependent:
in 1+1D the BW modular Hamiltonian asymptote is approximately recovered within a small-d1 window,
with a calculable suppression factor of approximately 1/30 as the remaining open question; in 3+1D the
BW asymptote is not recovered. The framework now lives as a horizon-thermodynamic interpretation
supplemented by a 1+1D modular-content match, with the dimensional dependence of the modularcontent match being the principal open problem. Section VI's small-d1 BW recovery in 1+1D, together
with the dimensional-contrast result in [11], identifies the prefactor calculation and the dimensionaldependence question as the sharpest research directions.

IX. METHODOLOGICAL NOTES
The calculations reported here were performed via autonomous runs of a large language model on
local hardware (NVIDIA RTX 5090, Intel Core Ultra 9, 256 GB DDR5) with full code execution and
persistence-to-disk capabilities. Three features of this methodology are worth highlighting.
First, validations against published results, not internal consistency checks alone, are the binding
constraint. The Calabrese–Cardy formula (10) and the Bombelli–Srednicki area-law coefficient (12) are
external benchmarks; agreement to better than 0.1% (1+1D) and a few percent (3+1D) is a meaningful
confirmation that the lattice implementations are correct. Pure symbolic or dimensional checks alone
cannot establish this. The methodological lesson from the C8 episode of [3] — dimensional consistency is
necessary but not sufficient — was applied here from the start.
Second, the falsifiability check (Section VII) is load-bearing. Without verifying that T_Unruh is
privileged in producing the comparison, the result could be dismissed as a tautology fixed by dimensional
analysis. The check rules this out: the test has empirical content.
Third, modular-content code requires special validation. The ΔK computation of Section VI passed
three internal cross-checks: relative-entropy positivity, modal-vs-original-basis agreement to six decimals,
and constant-offset consistency. An initial implementation produced relative-entropy violations of order
10⁴, an unphysical result that immediately flagged the implementation error. Correction yielded orderunity values with positivity verified throughout. Relative-entropy positivity is the right sanity check for
any modular-content code regardless of whether the underlying conjecture is true.

In addition, the 3+1D continuum-limit study was performed in two independent autonomous runs that
took different optimization paths: one used CPU NumPy with OpenBLAS multi-threading across 24 cores
and finished in approximately 48 minutes, while the other ported the eigendecomposition to GPU using
torch.linalg.eigh on the RTX 5090 and finished in under 3 minutes. The two runs agreed on ΔS to better
than 2×10⁻⁵ and on the area-law sequence S_vac/N² to four decimals. Independent code paths on
different hardware reaching the same numerical answer rules out implementation artifacts and round-offpattern artifacts simultaneously.

X. CONCLUSION
The literal static escrow postulate of [1], interpreted as a quantitative identification of S_esc = |
U_grav|/T_Unruh with QFT bipartition entanglement entropy, fails in both 1+1D and 3+1D. In 1+1D the
failure is qualitative (wrong sign, Dirichlet-wall behavior), quantitative (10.56 orders of magnitude in R
across the scan), and robust (lattice-size converged, invariant under localization-scheme choice, not a
dimensional artifact). In 3+1D the failure is structurally sharper: R_total has no finite continuum limit
because the bipartition entropy is dominated by the Bombelli–Srednicki area law, while S_esc remains Nindependent; the dimensionless mass-induced ratio R_Δ is bounded by 10⁻³ across the (L, m) grid. Two
independent code paths agree to five decimals on the 3+1D calculation. Mutual information confirms the
failure from a third independent direction (R_MI spans 12+ orders of magnitude).
One result is now positive within a finite regime: the modular Hamiltonian content ΔK, evaluated
under the Bisognano–Wichmann conjecture, approximately recovers the BW linear asymptote ΔK ∝ d1 in
1+1D within a small-d1 window (d1 ∈ [2, 6] at m=1), with a suppressed prefactor of approximately 1/30
of the literal BW value. The published v0.4/v0.5 framing of "sublinear scaling at exponent 0.7" is here
corrected to a regime-dependent characterization: the BW asymptote is recovered at small d1 with α ≈
1.0, the local exponent decreases smoothly through intermediate values, and a single power-law fit
averages across this crossover into the apparent 0.65–0.7 effective exponent. The 1+1D BW recovery is
the closest-to-positive empirical evidence in the lattice tests, with the prefactor calculation as the sharpest
open question (a candidate explanation in terms of UV/IR mismatch is discussed in Section VI.D). The
companion paper [11] reports that the analogous 3+1D test does not recover the BW asymptote within the
d1 range the lattice can resolve, with a peak ratio at matched mass approximately 14× smaller than the
1+1D peak ratio; whether 3+1D BW recovery can be achieved at much larger d1 inaccessible to the
present lattice remains an open question.
The published framework's interpretive content and empirical results are not refuted; what is ruled out
is the literal bipartition-entanglement reading of its central postulate. The framework's recovery of
Bekenstein–Hawking entropy at horizons, its deep-MOND asymptote, and its constant-a₀ empirical
content remain unaffected. Future tests should be directed at the modular-content reinterpretation, the
horizon limit directly, or curved-spacetime regimes that go beyond the flat-space lattice approach used
here.
The framework now lives as a sharper, narrower, and dimension-aware set of claims: horizon
thermodynamics is its strongest empirical anchor; flat-space bipartition entanglement is ruled out as a
literal identification in both 1+1D and 3+1D; modular Hamiltonian content approximately recovers the
BW linear asymptote in 1+1D within a small-d1 window (with a 1/30 prefactor whose origin is an open
theoretical question, plausibly attributable to UV/IR mismatch between the lattice-localized mass and the
continuum BW formula); modular-content recovery is dimension-dependent within the lattice sizes
presently accessible, with 3+1D failing to recover BW at the d1 range probed in the companion paper
[11]. Each of these is a bounded research direction with sharp positive-or-negative outcomes available
from future tests, including 2+1D as a natural intermediate dimensional case and N ≳ 20 GPU runs in
3+1D as a way to test whether BW recovery is reached at larger d1.
