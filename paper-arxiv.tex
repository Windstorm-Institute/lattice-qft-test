\begin{abstract}
We test the static gravitational entropy escrow postulate
$\Sesc = |U_{\mathrm{grav}}|/\Tu$ of Whitmer~[1] using lattice quantum field theory. In 1+1D, a
295-point scan of a free massless scalar (lattice sizes up to $N = 3000$) finds the dimensionless
ratio $R = \Sent/\Sesc$ spanning $10.56$ orders of magnitude; the mass-induced entanglement
$\Delta S = \Sent - \Svac$ is uniformly negative and lattice-converged. In 3+1D, an $N^{3}$-site scan
confirms the Bombelli--Srednicki area law ($\Svac/N^{2} \to 0.0228$) and shows that $R$ has no finite
continuum limit, because the bipartition entropy is dominated by the area-law vacuum term while the
mass-induced ratio $R_{\Delta}$ is bounded by $10^{-3}$; two independent code paths agree to five
decimals. Mutual information decays as $L^{-4}$, opposite to the linear growth the postulate requires.
The literal identification of $\Sesc$ with bipartition entanglement entropy is therefore ruled out in
both dimensions. The modular Hamiltonian content $\Delta K$, evaluated under the
Bisognano--Wichmann conjecture, shows a positive, approximately linear-in-$d_{1}$ window at $m=1$
(an empirical non-perturbative feature, \emph{not} a linear-response recovery of the BW asymptote---in
the linear-response regime $\Delta K$ is negative and $d_{1}$-flat), with prefactor $\approx 1/30$ of the
literal BW value ($\approx 1/7$ when energy-normalized). A companion paper~[11] finds no BW recovery
in 3+1D within the accessible $d_{1}$ range. The framework's horizon-limit recoveries are independent
of these flat-space tests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Gravitational Entropy Escrow framework~[1] proposes that gravitational binding energy is
identified with entropy held in escrow against the local Unruh temperature,
\begin{equation}
  \Sesc = \frac{|U_{\mathrm{grav}}|}{\Tu},
\end{equation}
where $U_{\mathrm{grav}}$ is the Newtonian binding energy of a configuration and
$\Tu = \hbar a/(2\pi c k_{B})$ is the Unruh temperature corresponding to the local acceleration $a$.
For two equal point masses $m$ at separation $L$, the prediction simplifies in lattice natural units
($c = \hbar = k_{B} = 1$, lattice spacing $= 1$) to
\begin{equation}
  \Sesc/k_{B} = 2\pi\,m\,L,
\end{equation}
with $G_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and the source mass cancelling identically. The framework recovers Newton's law
via the Verlinde construction~[2], Bekenstein--Hawking entropy at horizons via direct integration of
Eq.~(1) with surface gravity, and the deep-MOND acceleration scale
$a_{0} = c^{2}\sqrt{\Lambda/3}/(2\pi)$ from the de Sitter horizon-temperature floor.

The central claim of~[1] is interpretive: that $|U_{\mathrm{grav}}|/\Tu$ has the dimensions of, and
represents, an entropy held in escrow against the binding. A companion note~[3] ruled out the
natural-looking covariant local-current extension of Eq.~(1) as a notational reformulation of the
saturated Bekenstein bound~[4]. With the dynamical extension closed off, the static identification~(1)
becomes the load-bearing claim that requires independent testing.

This paper reports such a test. We compute several QFT entropy quantities of a free massless scalar in
the presence of two localized mass distributions and compare to the prediction~(2), in 1+1D using
Peschel's exact formula for Gaussian states~[5] and in 3+1D on cubic lattices. Three independent
entropy measures are tested: bipartition entanglement entropy, mutual information between regions, and
modular Hamiltonian content evaluated under the Bisognano--Wichmann conjecture~[6]. The headline
result is that the literal identification of $\Sesc$ with bipartition entanglement entropy fails in both
1+1D and 3+1D; the mutual information measure confirms this independently; and the modular
Hamiltonian content gives a partial result---a positive, approximately linear $\Delta K$ window at $m=1$
with the right shape but suppressed magnitude, and the opposite sign in the linear-response regime.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Lattice setup (1+1D)}
We discretize a free massless scalar field on a 1+1D lattice with $N$ sites and Dirichlet boundary
conditions at both walls (avoiding the periodic-BC zero mode). The Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
  H = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\bigl[\pi_{i}^{2} + (\phi_{i+1} - \phi_{i})^{2} + m_{i}^{2}\phi_{i}^{2}\bigr]
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\pi^{\mathsf{T}}\pi + \phi^{\mathsf{T}}K\phi\bigr],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  K = 2I - S - S^{\mathsf{T}} + \mathrm{diag}(m_{i}^{2}),
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the lattice shift operator. Two equal localized masses are placed at sites $(N-L)/2$ and
$(N+L)/2$, on opposite sides of the bipartition cut at site $N/2$ and at distance $L/2$ each from the
cut. Single-site delta masses are used; Section~IV.C verifies that the conclusions are invariant under
Gaussian smearing. The parameter scan covers $N \in \{100, 300, 1000, 3000\}$,
$L \in \{2,4,6,10,20,40,80,160,320,640\}$, and $m \in [10^{-3}, 10^{2}]$ sampled across orders of
magnitude, for 295 distinct $(N,L,m)$ points.

\subsection{Correlators and Peschel formula}
The ground state of~(3) is Gaussian, with closed-form correlators
\begin{equation}
  \langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}(K^{-1/2})_{ij},
  \qquad
  \langle\pi_{i}\pi_{j}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}(K^{1/2})_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Restricting to subsystem $A$ (sites $[0, N/2)$) gives reduced covariances
$M_{\phi} = \langle\phi\phi^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle|_{A}$ and
$M_{\pi} = \langle\pi\pi^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle|_{A}$. The symmetric product
$M_{\mathrm{sym}} = M_{\phi}^{1/2} M_{\pi} M_{\phi}^{1/2}$ has eigenvalues $\nu_{k}^{2} \ge \tfrac{1}{4}$,
and the bipartition entanglement entropy follows from Peschel's formula~[5]:
\begin{equation}
  \Sent = \sum_{k}\bigl[(\nu_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2})\ln(\nu_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2})
  - (\nu_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2})\ln(\nu_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Comparison metrics}
Three diagnostic ratios are computed and compared to the prediction~(2):
\begin{equation}
  \Rtot = \Sent/\Sesc,
  \qquad
  R_{\Delta} = \Delta S/\Sesc,
  \qquad
  \Delta S \equiv \Sent - \Svac,
\end{equation}
where $\Svac$ is the entanglement entropy at the same $N$ with no localized masses. If the postulate~(2)
holds with an order-unity prefactor, the diagnostic ratios should be approximately constant of order
unity across the scan. Mutual information $I(M_{1}{:}M_{2})$ and modular content $\Delta K$ are
introduced in Sections~V and~VI.

\subsection{Lattice setup (3+1D)}
The 3+1D setup uses a cubic lattice of $N^{3}$ sites with Dirichlet boundary conditions on all six
faces. Two point masses are placed on the central $x$-axis at $(i, N/2, N/2)$ with $i = (N\pm L)/2$, the
bipartition cutting the cube at $i = N/2$. The $K$-matrix has shape $N^{3}\times N^{3}$; for $N=20$ this
is $8000\times 8000$. The scan covers $N \in \{10,12,14,16,18,20\}$, $L \in \{2,4,6,8\}$, and
$m \in \{0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$. Two independent computational paths were used---a CPU implementation
(NumPy/OpenBLAS, 24 cores) and a GPU implementation (\texttt{torch.linalg.eigh}, NVIDIA RTX 5090)---agreeing
on $\Delta S$ at the $N=14$ anchor to better than $2\times 10^{-5}$.

Three validations against published results were performed before any scan was treated as established.

\subsection{Heavy-mass Dirichlet limit (V2)}
As $m\to\infty$ the localized-mass sites become perfect Dirichlet walls, and Calabrese--Cardy predicts
$\Sent(m\to\infty) \to (1/6)\ln(2L/\pi) + \mathrm{const}$. For $L=20$: predicted $S \to 0.394$; measured
at $m=100$: $S = 0.394$, agreement to four decimal places---an internal cross-check that the
localized-mass machinery and the Peschel reduction agree at the analytically tractable limit.

\subsection{Symbolic dimensional consistency (V3)}
The escrow prediction~(2) was derived symbolically (sympy) and verified to be the unique
dimensionally-consistent form after cancellation of $G_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and the source mass. Per the
lessons of~[3], dimensional consistency is necessary but not sufficient; the substantive checks are V1
and V2.

\subsection{3+1D area law (V4)}

\subsection{Why the literal postulate fails: the structural mismatch}
The escrow prediction~(2) grows linearly with both $m$ and $L$ without bound. The free-scalar
entanglement entropy behaves very differently: the vacuum part $\Svac$ scales as $(1/6)\ln(2N/\pi)$
(bounded for fixed $N$), and the mass-induced part $\Delta S$ is bounded above by $O(1)$ nats---localized
masses entangle or disentangle modes only within a correlation length $\xi\sim 1/m$, giving an $O(1)$
contribution that saturates as $L\to\infty$. Heavy masses act as effective Dirichlet walls, decreasing
the bipartition entanglement and giving $\Delta S < 0$. The mismatch is structural, not a missing
prefactor: $\Sesc$ is extensive ($\propto mL$) while $\Delta S$ is intensive and bounded. No constant
rescaling or modified temperature can reconcile a bounded quantity with an unbounded one.

\subsection{Robustness against UV artifacts}
The result is invariant under the mass implementation. Single-site delta masses and Gaussian-smeared
profiles with widths $\sigma \in \{1,2,5\}$ all give the same qualitative behavior: $\Delta S$ uniformly
negative, $\Rtot$ varying across many orders of magnitude. For $m\le 0.1$, $\Delta S$ is invariant to
smearing to better than $1\%$; for $m\gtrsim 1$, smearing makes $\Delta S$ more negative (a broader
``wall'' removes more entanglement). Single-site UV artifacts are not driving the result.

\section{Independent confirmations}
\subsection{Mutual information $I(M_{1}{:}M_{2})$}
A natural alternative measure is the mutual information between regions surrounding the two masses,
\begin{equation}
  I(M_{1}{:}M_{2}) = S(M_{1}) + S(M_{2}) - S(M_{1}\cup M_{2}).
\end{equation}
For free massless scalars in 1+1D the leading contribution at large separation scales as~[10]
$I(M_{1}{:}M_{2}) \sim (w/L)^{4}$ with $w$ the region width. We measured it for $w=4$ and
$L \in \{10,20,40,80,160,320\}$: the mutual information decays as $L^{-4}$, while the postulate predicts
growth $\propto 2\pi m L$. The ratio $R_{\mathrm{MI}}$ thus spans more than 12 orders of magnitude in
the opposite direction to what the postulate requires---an independent confirmation.

\subsection{3+1D continuum-limit study}
The 3+1D bipartition entanglement entropy was computed across $N \in \{10,\dots,20\}$; the vacuum
area-law validation (Table~II) confirmed the implementation. The diagnostic ratio $\Rtot$ has no finite
continuum limit---it grows as $N^{2}$ across the entire $(L,m)$ grid, with
\begin{equation}
  \Rtot\cdot\Sesc = \Svac
  \quad\text{[to better than 1\% at every point; residual is the $R_{\Delta}\lesssim 10^{-3}$ mass effect].}
\end{equation}
The reason is transparent: the 3+1D bipartition entropy is dominated by the area-law vacuum
contribution $\Svac \propto N^{2}$, unrelated to the mass binding, while $\Sesc = 2\pi m L$ is
$N$-independent.

\section{Modular Hamiltonian content: a suggestive window in 1+1D}
\subsection{Bisognano--Wichmann motivation}
The Bisognano--Wichmann theorem~[6] states that the modular Hamiltonian $K_{A}$ of a
Lorentz-invariant QFT restricted to a half-space is, in vacuum,
\begin{equation}
  K_{\mathrm{BW}} = 2\pi\int x_{\perp}\,T_{00}(x)\,d^{d}x,
\end{equation}
with $x_{\perp}$ the perpendicular distance from the entangling surface. For a localized energy $m$ at
distance $L$, the linear-response prediction is $\Delta K_{\mathrm{BW}} \simeq 2\pi m L$, exactly the form
of the escrow prediction~(2). This structural match raises the possibility that the postulate's correct
identification is with modular Hamiltonian content rather than entanglement entropy.

\subsection{Lattice implementation}
We computed
$\Delta K = \langle K_{A}^{\mathrm{vac}}\rangle_{\mathrm{pert}} -
\langle K_{A}^{\mathrm{vac}}\rangle_{\mathrm{vac}}$ across the same 1+1D grid. Using the Williamson
normal form of the vacuum covariance, with modular eigenvalues
$\varepsilon_{k} = \ln[(\nu_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2})/(\nu_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2})]$, the quadratic modular kernels are
$Q = M_{\phi}^{-1/2} V\,\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_{k}\nu_{k})\,V^{\mathsf{T}} M_{\phi}^{-1/2}$ and
$P = M_{\phi}^{1/2} V\,\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_{k}/\nu_{k})\,V^{\mathsf{T}} M_{\phi}^{1/2}$, so that
$\langle K_{A}\rangle = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(Q M'_{\phi}) + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(P M'_{\pi})$. The
implementation was validated by three checks: relative-entropy positivity
$S_{\mathrm{rel}} = \Delta K + \Svac - S_{\mathrm{pert}} \ge 0$ for all states; a modal-vs-original-basis
cross-check agreeing to six decimals; and constant-offset consistency with $\langle K_{A}\rangle_{\mathrm{vac}} = \Svac$.

\subsection{Result: right form, wrong magnitude}
In the $m\approx 1$ window the lattice $\Delta K$ reproduces the BW structural shape (a non-perturbative feature, opposite to the negative $\Delta K$ of small-$m$ linear response): it is positive, grows with $L$, and is
approximately linear in $m$ for moderate values. Two discrepancies emerge. First, the $L$-scaling is
not a single power law. A sliding-window analysis over $d_{1}$ (the perpendicular distance of the mass
in $A$ to the cut, $=L/2$ in the symmetric placement) shows the local exponent evolving continuously
from $\Delta K \propto d_{1}^{1.02\pm 0.05}$ at small $d_{1}\in[2,6]$ (indistinguishable from the BW
linear asymptote) down to $\Delta K \propto d_{1}^{0.5\pm 0.04}$ in the decay tail $d_{1}\in[16,64]$. The
previously-published single exponent ``$0.7$'' was an artifact of fitting one power law across a smooth
crossover, and is retracted as a universal description.

Second, the magnitude is smaller than the literal BW prediction---though part of the gap is a
normalization convention: the $\tfrac{1}{2}m^{2}\phi_{i}^{2}$ site perturbation inserts ground-state
energy $\Delta E \approx 0.256$ at $m=1$, and normalizing $\Delta K$ to $\Delta E$ rather than to $m$
raises the prefactor from $\approx 1/30$ to $\approx 1/7$. In the small-$d_{1}$ BW regime
($d_{1}\in[2,6]$, $m=1$, $N=1000$), the prefactor $\Delta K/(2\pi m d_{1})$ is $\approx 0.030$--$0.037$,
a suppression of roughly $1/30$ relative to the literal BW value. At heavier mass the apparent ratio
drops further, as $\Delta K$ saturates rather than continuing to grow as $2\pi m d_{1}$; the positive,
approximately linear-in-$d_1$ window we report sits at $m\lesssim 1$ and $d_{1}\in[2,8]$. This window
is an empirical non-perturbative feature, not the small-mass linear-response regime---in genuine
linear response ($m\ll 1$), $\Delta K$ is negative and $d_1$-flat.

\subsection{Interpretation}
The 1+1D modular result is consistent with the BW asymptote at small $d_{1}$ with a suppressed
prefactor. The smooth crossover into a sublinear tail at $d_{1}\gtrsim 8$ reflects geometric corrections
to the BW half-space assumption (Dirichlet rather than free boundary conditions; finite subsystem-$A$
depth), consistent with a finite-size deformation of BW rather than a fundamental failure. A substantial
part of the prefactor is a normalization convention (energy-normalizing gives $\approx 1/7$); whether the
residual suppression has a calculable origin is left for future work. We emphasize that this is a
suggestive-but-inconclusive result: it is an empirical non-perturbative feature at $m=1$, \emph{not} a
controlled linear-response recovery of BW---in the linear-response regime $\Delta K$ is negative and
$d_{1}$-flat.

A candidate explanation worth flagging is a UV/IR mismatch: the BW formula is an infrared prediction
valid in the continuum, whereas a lattice mass with $m\approx 1$ has correlation length
$\xi\sim 1/m\sim 1$ lattice spacing---a UV-scale perturbation. The $1/30$ suppression may reflect this
mismatch and could in principle be computed by evaluating the lattice modular Hamiltonian in the
continuum scaling limit. A second candidate is that the Dirichlet boundary conditions used on the lattice differ from the free boundary conditions assumed in the standard BW derivation, which may contribute a calculable boundary-correction factor to the residual prefactor. The dimensional dependence is investigated in a companion paper~[11], which
finds the 3+1D BW asymptote is \emph{not} recovered within the resolvable $d_{1}$ range: $\Delta K$
peaks at $d_{1}\approx 2$ and decays as $d_{1}^{-2\text{ to }-3}$, with a matched-mass peak ratio
$\approx 0.0026$, about $14\times$ smaller than the 1+1D peak $\approx 0.037$. The companion paper notes that the rate of approach to the continuum BW asymptote may genuinely be slower in 3+1D---where the conformal-symmetry group of the massless free scalar is finite-dimensional---than in 1+1D, where it is the infinite-dimensional Virasoro algebra; 3+1D BW recovery at much larger $d_{1}$ inaccessible to the present lattice therefore cannot be strictly ruled out. We do not adjudicate
among these readings; the result is reported as the most promising direction for future modification of
the postulate and as a sharp constraint on what such modifications must satisfy.

\section{Falsifiability check}
A central concern is that any apparent agreement could be a tautology fixed by the choice of
dimensional combinations. The Unruh temperature varies as $\Tu \propto m/L^{2}$, spanning five orders
of magnitude across the scan. We verified that no choice of $T$ produces a uniform $R\simeq 1$ across
the scan; $\Tu$ is not privileged in this sense. The $10.56$ orders of magnitude variation in $R$ is
therefore a real measurement of the disagreement, not an artifact of dimensional rescaling. The Section~IV
result is a genuine quantitative measurement of the postulate's failure as bipartition entanglement
entropy.

\section{What this settles, and what it does not}
\textbf{What this settles.} The literal static escrow postulate~(1), as a quantitative identification of
$\Sesc$ with QFT bipartition entanglement entropy in flat space, does not survive in either 1+1D or
3+1D. In 1+1D the qualitative behavior is wrong (sign, scaling, boundedness in $N$), the magnitude is
wrong ($10.56$ orders of scatter), and lattice-size convergence rules out finite-size explanations. In
3+1D the bipartition entropy is dominated by the area-law vacuum term and has no finite continuum
limit; the mass-induced ratio $R_{\Delta}$ is bounded by $10^{-3}$. Two code paths agree to five
decimals, and mutual information confirms the failure from a third direction.

\textbf{What this does not settle.} The horizon limit is unaffected: the recovery of Bekenstein--Hawking
entropy~[1,\,\S5] goes through surface gravity and the Unruh temperature at the horizon directly, not
through volume-bipartition entanglement. Curved-spacetime regimes are outside scope. The framework's
empirical content---the constant-$a_{0}$ SPARC reanalysis, the deep-MOND asymptote, and the baryonic
Tully--Fisher relation---does not depend on the literal bipartition-entanglement reading. Modular-content
variants remain partially open: the 1+1D window at $m=1$ (not a linear-response recovery; $\Delta K$ is
negative and flat in linear response) with prefactor $\approx 1/30$, and the companion paper's 3+1D
non-recovery, show the modular-content interpretation has dimension-dependent validity within currently
accessible lattice sizes.

\section{Methodological notes}
The calculations were performed via autonomous runs on local hardware (NVIDIA RTX 5090, Intel Core
Ultra 9, 256~GB DDR5) with full code execution. Three features are worth highlighting. First,
validations against published results (Calabrese--Cardy, Bombelli--Srednicki), not internal consistency
checks alone, are the binding constraint---agreement to better than $0.1\%$ (1+1D) and a few percent
(3+1D) confirms the implementations. Second, the falsifiability check (Section~VII) is load-bearing:
without verifying that $\Tu$ is privileged, the result could be dismissed as a tautology. Third,
modular-content code requires special validation: the $\Delta K$ computation passed relative-entropy
positivity, modal-vs-original-basis agreement to six decimals, and constant-offset consistency; an initial
implementation produced order-$10^{4}$ relative-entropy violations that immediately flagged an error in
the Williamson decomposition. The 3+1D study was run on two independent paths (CPU NumPy,
$\sim$48~min; GPU PyTorch, $<$3~min) agreeing on $\Delta S$ to better than $2\times 10^{-5}$, ruling out
implementation and round-off artifacts.

\section{Conclusion}
The literal static escrow postulate of~[1], interpreted as a quantitative identification of
$\Sesc = |U_{\mathrm{grav}}|/\Tu$ with QFT bipartition entanglement entropy, fails in both 1+1D and
3+1D. In 1+1D the failure is qualitative (wrong sign, Dirichlet-wall behavior), quantitative ($10.56$
orders of magnitude in $R$), and robust; in 3+1D it is structurally sharper---$\Rtot$ has no finite
continuum limit because the bipartition entropy is dominated by the Bombelli--Srednicki area law, and
$R_{\Delta}$ is bounded by $10^{-3}$. Two code paths agree to five decimals, and mutual information
confirms the failure independently.

One result is positive within a finite regime: the modular Hamiltonian content $\Delta K$, under the
Bisognano--Wichmann conjecture, shows a positive, approximately linear window ($\Delta K\propto d_{1}$)
at $m=1$---resembling but not controllably recovering the BW asymptote (in linear response $\Delta K$ is
negative and $d_{1}$-flat)---in a small-$d_{1}$ window, with a suppressed prefactor $\approx 1/30$ of the
literal BW value ($\approx 1/7$ energy-normalized). The published ``sublinear scaling at exponent 0.7''
framing is corrected to a regime-dependent characterization. The companion paper~[11] reports that the
analogous 3+1D test does not recover BW within the resolvable $d_{1}$ range. The published framework's
interpretive content and empirical results are not refuted; what is ruled out is the literal
bipartition-entanglement reading of its central postulate. Future tests should target the modular-content
reinterpretation, the horizon limit directly, or curved-spacetime regimes---including 2+1D as an
intermediate case and $N\gtrsim 20$ GPU runs in 3+1D to test whether BW recovery is reached at larger
$d_{1}$.
